# Finite search, 26 November 2026

The previous proof and experiment files are preserved. The earlier block and wheel tests primarily inspected t ≥ 0. Put u=−t and close a series chain of two-terminal networks into a ring. For u>0 the connected-terminal contribution dominates. Two networks with cancelling first derivatives in u can produce a finite violation without a growing ear. Tests below investigate this family; all exploratory floating-point results require exact certification.

First construction found: a ring containing nine squares and seven copies of K4 minus its terminal edge. Opposite square vertices are the terminals. Writing C for the connected-terminal sum and P for the individual Tutte polynomial at (1−u,1+u), the ring value is [(1+u²)∏C−∏P]/u. The C polynomials are 4−u and 8+3u+u². An initial screen gives negative second derivative at u=1. The next bounded search varies the two-terminal blocks on at most six vertices, using this construction as a size cutoff.

The exact search at the endpoint x=0 found a 19-vertex graph with 30 edges. Its core is K5 and its second terminal-to-terminal route is five squares in series. The whole-graph coefficient triple at x=0 from deletion–contraction is (280024,5612,−3278). Deleting one edge or contracting one edge and simplifying parallel copies gave no negative quadratic coefficient among the resulting simple 2-connected graphs. This check concerns one endpoint and supplies no minimality conclusion.

For the size screen I retained distinct coefficient triples through degree two of C and P for labelled connected simple networks on 2 through 6 vertices whose completion by a terminal edge has no cut vertex. I tested two block types in rings with at most 12 cells and at most 19 total vertices, omitting the degenerate two-edge dipole. The primary graph was the endpoint hit. The scan used integer Taylor coefficients and identified equivalent coefficient data, so it was not a census of graphs. Existing positive-side searches were not rerun.

For an elementary strict-peak example, allowing more cells gives a planar ring of 19 squares and 15 squares with one internal diagonal. Exact evaluation at x=0,1/8,1/4 gives a strict interior excess on both comparisons. The finite examples suffice for the requested simplification, so the size search stopped here. Hiraishi et al. (2019) remains an unresolved priority lead.
