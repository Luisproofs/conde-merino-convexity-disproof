# A counterexample to the Conde–Merino convexity conjecture

Research manuscript, 26 November 2026. The 2019 Hiraishi et al. proceedings paper is a material unresolved priority lead. The mathematical claim below is independent of priority. See review.md.

The precise claim in Conde and Merino, *Comparing the Number of Acyclic and Totally Cyclic Orientations with That of Spanning Trees of a Graph*, International J. Math. Combin. 2 (2009), 79–89, Conjecture 2.4, is as follows. For every 2-connected simple graph G with no loops, the restriction of its Tutte polynomial T(G;x,y) to the entire closed line segment from (2,0) to (0,2) is convex. Equivalently P_G(t)=T_G(1+t,1−t) must be convex for −1≤t≤1. [Published paper](https://fs.unm.edu/IJMC/ComparingTheNumberOfAcyclicAndTotallyCyclicOrientations.pdf)

**Theorem.** There are infinitely many finite simple 2-vertex-connected graphs G for which the function P_G(t) = T_G(1+t,1-t) has an interior value strictly greater than both ends of a subinterval of (0,1). The subinterval can be taken arbitrarily close to zero on the right. In particular Conjecture 2.4 is false.

## 1. Substitution identity

For a connected graph K and spanning edge subset A, put k(A) equal to its number of components, including isolated vertices, and j(A)=|A|-|V(K)|+k(A). The subset expansion is the sum of t^{k(A)-1}(-t)^{j(A)}. The two-terminal graph N used below is K6 with a single edge removed. Its terminals are the two endpoints of the removed edge. Define

    C(t) = sum over terminal-connected A of t^{k(A)-1}(-t)^{j(A)},
    D(t) = sum over terminal-separated A of t^{k(A)-2}(-t)^{j(A)}.

For a connected core H with m edges and rank r=|V(H)|-1, replace its edges by fresh copies of N attached at their terminals, obtaining B. Then

    P_B(t) = C(t)^r D(t)^{m-r} T_H(1+t D(t)/C(t), 1-t C(t)/D(t)).

Indeed, fix the set J of core edges whose terminals are connected in their respective copies. There are k_H(J) components meeting core vertices. Each terminal-connected copy contributes k(A)-1 additional components and each separated copy contributes k(A)-2. The cycle rank of the union is the sum of the local cycle ranks plus j_H(J), by subtracting vertices from edges and adding components. Thus the partial sum over this J is t^{k_H(J)-1}(-t)^{j_H(J)} C^{|J|}D^{m-|J|}. Factoring the displayed powers gives the identity whenever C and D are nonzero.

The first three coefficients in increasing powers of t are

| polynomial | constant | linear | quadratic |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| D | 432 | −312 | 372 |
| C | 864 | −1836 | 2622 |

These six integers are small finite sums. To reproduce them, enumerate subsets of the 14 unordered pairs of vertices of {0,...,5} other than {0,1}. Count components by joining the endpoints of each chosen pair. Add (-1)^j to the entry in column k-1+j if 0 and 1 are connected, or k-2+j otherwise, in the corresponding row. Terms of degree greater than two have no effect. In particular C,D are positive near zero.

## 2. The core and its limiting polynomial evaluations

Take a sequence of simple 2-connected cubic graphs H with girth tending to infinity. One elementary existence construction uses the group with three involutory generators and no further relations. For each integer g, take the ball of reduced words of length at most g. On this finite set each generator pairs words joined by an edge of that label inside the ball and fixes the remaining unmatched words. These three permutations are involutions. Every nonempty reduced word of length at most g moves the empty word, since its trajectory spells a reduced path within the ball (the reverse reading convention gives the reversed word). The finite permutation group generated by the involutions therefore has a connected cubic Cayley graph with no cycle of length at most g, for g at least 2. Its generators are distinct and nonidentity. The Cayley graph is vertex-transitive and has no cut vertex: endpoints of a longest simple path in a finite connected graph are non-cut vertices, and vertex transitivity transfers this property to every vertex.

Write n=|V(H)|, so r=n-1 and m=3n/2. For any fixed sufficiently small t>0 set X=1+tD/C and Y=1-tC/D. Then 1<X<2 and 0<Y<1. Theorem 1.3 of Ferenc Bencs and Péter Csikvári gives

    lim T_H(X,Y)^{1/n} = 2 (4/(4-X))^{1/2}.

[Large-girth Tutte theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.06798)

Consequently the pointwise limit of log P_B(t)/n on a right neighborhood of zero is the real-analytic function

    L(t) = log C(t) + (1/2)log D(t) + log 2 + (1/2)log(4/(4-X(t))).

The derivatives below are derivatives of this explicit function. No interchange of a limit with a derivative is needed. From the table, X'(0)=1/2 and X''(0)=2(D'/C-DC'/C²) at zero. The log derivative formulas give

    L'(0) = -173/72,
    L''(0) = 12451/5184,
    L''(0)+L'(0) = -5/5184.

For example the contribution from −(1/2)log(4-X) is X'/6 to L' and X''/6+(X')²/18 to L'' at 0. Each quadratic table entry is half a second derivative.

## 3. Turning log curvature into an actual failure of convexity

Choose t₀>0 sufficiently close to zero that L'(t₀)<0 and L''(t₀)+L'(t₀)/(1+t₀)<0. Set λ=−(1+t₀)L'(t₀)>0. The function

    M(t) = L(t)+λ log(1+t)

has M'(t₀)=0 and M''(t₀)<0. Fix h>0 small enough that 0<t₀−h<t₀+h<1, all three points belong to the neighborhood above, and M(t₀)>max(M(t₀−h),M(t₀+h)).

For each B add a path of length ℓ=max(2,⌊λn⌋) with new internal vertices between the ends of an edge e joining two internal vertices in one copy of N. Let the resulting graph be G. For x=1+t, y=1−t, deletion–contraction gives

    P_G(t) = (1+x+...+x^{ℓ−1}) P_B(t) + T_{B/st}(x,y),

where B/st identifies the endpoints of e retaining every edge, including the resulting loop. To see the formula, delete the first edge of the new path to leave ℓ−1 bridges attached to B, or contract it to shorten the path. At length one the contraction term identifies the endpoints. Because e is an ordinary edge, T_{B/st}=y T_{B/e} ≤ y T_B, by nonnegativity and deletion–contraction on e. Thus at each fixed t under consideration

    lim log P_G(t)/n = M(t).

Convergence at the three fixed points gives, for all sufficiently large cores in the sequence, P_G(t₀)>max(P_G(t₀−h),P_G(t₀+h)). This contradicts the midpoint inequality required by convexity.

Finally B is simple since the inserted networks are simple and have disjoint internal vertices, with no edge directly between their terminals. Deleting a core vertex leaves the rest of the core connected through the undamaged networks, and every incident network remains connected to its surviving terminal. Deleting an internal vertex leaves its network connected because K6 minus one edge remains connected after any single deletion. The added path preserves 2-connectivity: deleting its internal vertex leaves both pieces attached to B, and deleting an endpoint leaves the rest of the path attached at the other end. Each core vertex has degree 12, since three copies each contribute four incident edges. The internal vertices of a copy have degree five, increasing to six at the two ear endpoints. All new internal vertices have degree two. Thus the counterexamples even have maximum degree 12. The sizes are unbounded as the girth of the cores increases. This proves the theorem.

## Consequence

The section of the Tutte polynomial along x+y=2 can fail even quasiconvexity on simple 2-connected graphic examples. Its value can strictly exceed the midpoint chord on an interval lying wholly to the right of (1,1). Thus convexity cannot provide the proposed universal proof of the Merino–Welsh spanning-tree inequality. The latter compares only (2,0), (1,1), and (0,2), and its resolution remains open in this investigation.

## 4. A fixed rational certificate

There is a single choice of path lengths and evaluation points for the entire infinite family. Take λ=216250003/90000000 and, in increasing order, a=1/1000000, b=1/25000, c=1/10000. With the same M as above, exact rational enclosures give

    60 × 10⁻¹⁴ < M(b) − M(a) < 61 × 10⁻¹⁴,
    94 × 10⁻¹⁴ < M(b) − M(c) < 95 × 10⁻¹⁴.

To verify, the complete coefficient lists, starting with the constants, are

    D = [432, −312, 372, −196, 82, −20, 2],
    C = [864, −1836, 2622, −2569, 1840, −963, 360, −91, 14, −1].

Use R(t)=16D(t)C(t)³/(3C(t)−tD(t)), so that exp(2L(t))=R(t). For z>0 put u=(z−1)/(z+1). The first four terms of 2 times the series for arctanh(u) approximate log z with error of absolute value at most 2|u|⁹/[9(1−u²)]. Apply this to R(b)/R(a) and (1+b)/(1+a), with respective multipliers 1/2 and λ, then repeat with c in place of a. The calculation, including the hypotheses for Theorem 1.3 at the three evaluation points, is reproduced by verify_convexity.py.

In particular for every sufficiently large core, P_G(b) exceeds both P_G(a) and P_G(c). The ratio of the interior value to the larger endpoint grows exponentially in the number of core vertices. This is stronger than failure of convexity by a bounded additive error. No explicit upper bound on the first counterexample size is asserted.
