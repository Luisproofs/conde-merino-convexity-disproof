See [the preprint](preprint/conde-merino.pdf), [finite examples](finite/proof.md), [planar examples](finite/planar.md), and [current Lean verification](lean/README.md) for the subsequent results. The priority search remains incomplete; the 2019 paper is unresolved.

**Mathematical result.** The manuscript disproves Conde and Merino's Conjecture 2.4 on its published graph class. All sufficiently large members of a specified infinite sequence have a strict interior peak on a fixed subinterval of the segment x+y=2. The ratio to the larger endpoint of that subinterval tends to infinity. **Historical priority is unresolved.** The full 2019 paper discussed below is the most consequential outstanding evidence.

## History, importance and scope

Merino and Welsh's 1999 graph question compares the evaluations (2,0), (1,1), (0,2). Conde and Merino published their convexity conjecture in 2009 as a proposed explanation for that inequality. It imposes the convexity inequality at *every* pair of points on the segment, including intervals that miss (1,1). Chávez-Lomelí, Merino, Noble and Ramírez-Ibáñez proved convexity along slope-minus-one segments for coloopless paving matroids. Ok obtained the convexity result for minimally 2-edge-connected graphs. These partial theorems illustrate why a universal structural proof was a serious possibility. [Published conjecture](https://fs.unm.edu/IJMC/ComparingTheNumberOfAcyclicAndTotallyCyclicOrientations.pdf) · [Paving-matroid theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.2639) · [Ok's result](https://www.imada.sdu.dk/u/jbj/GT2015/ok.pdf)

All the counterexamples can have maximum degree 12. They rule out every universal right-neighborhood of (1,1) on which all such graphs have quasiconvex restrictions. They also exclude any constant-factor substitute bounding the interior value by the larger subinterval endpoint on the full graph class. This follows from the strictly separated limiting log evaluations. The statements concern fixed rational interior points or sufficiently small subintervals. The active original endpoint conjecture has separate hypotheses and a separate comparison. [Recent work on that conjecture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15008)

The mathematical advance over the previous benchmark, if priority clears, would be a connection between a classical graph-polynomial problem and a large-girth limit, with an operation turning a local feature of the limit into exponentially large violations for actual graphs. The earlier McNamara–Steingrímsson result, as described by the requester, has small explicit witnesses and an infinite family (not Lean-formalized). The present proof uses a published asymptotic theorem and gives no numerical order for its first counterexample. Neither professional reception nor a higher significance ranking can be established from this investigation. The convexity question was selected during the endpoint investigation because it is an independently stated strengthening with a distinct proof strategy.

## Adversarial checks

1. **Independent finite calculations.** Direct enumeration separates the 16,384 subsets by terminal connectivity. A second algorithm uses deletion–contraction with independent loop and bridge rules on N and on its terminal identification. The identities P_N=C+tD and P_identified=D−tC recover the two local polynomials uniquely over the rationals. The algorithms agree on every coefficient. A third count, by the labelled component of a specified vertex, is implemented in clique_network.py.
2. **Signs and normalization.** The normalized limit uses the number of vertices of the cubic core. Its edge density is 3/2. The core Tutte arguments multiply to (X−1)(Y−1)=−t² as required. The external result is Theorem 1.3 of Bencs and Csikvári. Only its deterministically large-girth branch with 1<X<2 and 0<Y<1 is used. [Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.06798)
3. **Small negative margin.** The derivative sum −5/5184 is exact. Separately the fixed rational certificate proves the two log-rate gaps positive using truncated series with rigorous rational remainders. Both computations run in verify_convexity.py.
4. **Transfer of strict inequalities.** Negative curvature of a normalized log alone would be insufficient. The added path cancels the first derivative at an interior point. Comparison at three fixed points then transfers a strict interior maximum to finite polynomial values. There is no assertion that derivatives converge.
5. **Graph class.** The six-vertex network has no edge between its terminals. All internal vertices are fresh. The core, every replacement after one deletion, and the ear satisfy the vertex-deletion checks in the proof. The loop from identifying adjacent vertices is confined to an auxiliary polynomial.
6. **Verification boundary.** The algorithms are independent checks of the arithmetic within this investigation. No external mathematician has reviewed this work here. The paper proof relies on the published large-girth theorem. No Lean formalization or finite counterexample adjacency list is claimed.

## Lean 4 formalization strategy

Encode finite edge-labelled graphs allowing loops and parallel edges for intermediate minors. Prove the component and nullity formulas for replacing edges by a fixed two-terminal network. Implement the six-vertex sum with a decidable connectivity predicate and certify the integer polynomial identities by normalization. Prove the ear identity via deletion–contraction and certify the rational log enclosures using the arctanh series bound, or use the exact derivative sign and continuity. Construct the finite permutation Cayley cores and prove the simplicity, girth and cut-vertex statements. Isolate Theorem 1.3 of Bencs–Csikvári as a named dependency and audit whether a formal proof exists. A kernel-complete disproof via this route would require proving or importing that theorem. A smaller concrete graph with a certified negative midpoint difference would permit a wholly finite alternative. Finally prove the pointwise three-point limit lemma and instantiate it. Formalization has been deferred.
